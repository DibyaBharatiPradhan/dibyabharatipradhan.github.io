About me

I work on the atmospheres of rocky exoplanets. I use climate, photochemical and retrieval models to understand which gases future missions such as HWO and LIFE could detect, and what they would tell us about a planet's habitability and possible signs of life.

I approach this from several directions. I have used 3D climate models to simulate planets around M dwarfs, such as Proxima Centauri b and TRAPPIST-1e, and the transition of early Venus towards a runaway greenhouse. With photochemical and radiative transfer models, I studied how surface conditions control the detectability of biosignature gases such as O3, CH4 and N2O (Pradhan et al. 2026, ApJ). More recently, I have worked on Bayesian atmospheric retrievals for the LIFE mission, testing how model assumptions bias what we infer from the spectra of Earth-like planets.

I hold a five-year integrated M.Sc. in Physics from the National Institute of Science Education and Research (NISER), India, and have since worked on exoplanet atmospheres at ETH Zurich and with collaborators at the University of Bern. I am now looking for a Ph.D. position where I can study biosignatures, their false positives and planetary habitability in depth.

Current Research

Earth Through Time with LIFE

Assessing Biases in Atmospheric Retrievals of Terrestrial Worlds with Large Interferometer for Exoplanets (LIFE). Investigating LIFE's ability to characterize habitable exoplanets across Earth's evolutionary stages (Prebiotic, GOE, NOE, Modern) using simulated mid-IR thermal emission spectra and developing retrieval pipelines to quantify how more physically realistic atmospheric models improve biosignature detectability and parameter constraints.

Supervised by Prof. Sascha P. Quanz (ETH Zurich)

Past Projects (during undergrad and masters)

Atmospheric Biosignatures in Earth Analogs

Simulated Earth-like atmospheres for direct imaging observations in upcoming missions like HWO and LIFE, focusing on the identification of molecular features visible in reflection and thermal emission spectra. Explored prospects for characterization in the UV, Visible, Near-infrared, and Mid-infrared Regions.

Supervised by Dr. Liton Majumdar (NISER)

Modeling the climate of Terrestrial planets with the UM, a 3D GCM

Simulating the atmospheres of terrestrial planets and investigating their dynamics with the Met Office Unified Model (UM), a 3D General Circulation Model (GCM). Benchmarked with TRAPPIST-1e and Proxima Centauri b for different atmospheric compositions and simulated potential temperate climatic conditions of early Venus.

Supervised by Dr. Jayesh M Goyal (NISER)

Study of Stellar Structures

Simulated self-gravitating equilibria (e.g., white dwarfs and neutron stars), using Polytropic and Isothermal models followed by the statistical mechanics of these systems. Explored Gravothermal Collapse and the impact of Special Relativity on Isothermal Spheres.

Supervised by Dr. Dawood Kothawala (IIT Madras)
